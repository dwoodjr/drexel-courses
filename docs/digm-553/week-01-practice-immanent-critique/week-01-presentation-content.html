W01A — The Problem

The Prebifurcation Point

Most institutional models treat research and creative practice as sequential: research produces knowledge, practice illustrates it.

Research-creation names the activity that happens before this split — where making and thinking are not distinct acts but two registers of the same act, happening simultaneously.

Manning & Massumi: activity at the prebifurcation point — before research and creation diverge into institutional structures that evaluate them by separate criteria.

W01A — Immanent Critique

Inhabiting Complicity

"An immanent critique engages with new processes more than new products… The strategy of immanent critique is to inhabit one's complicity and make it turn — in the sense in which butter 'turns' to curd." — Manning & Massumi

We work within the university, with commercial tools, toward grades. That is our complicity. Immanent critique means making that complicity productive — not waiting until we're free of it.

W01A — Three Distinctions

Distinctions to Internalize

01

Process, not Product

"An interpenetration of processes rather than a communication of products." The artifact is the record of practice, not the point of it.

02

Conditioning, not Framing

Framing sets rules. Conditioning brings co-causes into interaction so that something emerges that neither cause alone could produce.

03

Enabling Constraints

A limitation that, through interaction, generates emergence rather than restriction. Gravity enables the jump.

W01A — Enabling Constraints

The Productive Limit

"In our experience, unconstrained interaction rarely yields worthwhile effects." — Manning & Massumi

The MPR121 sensor constrains what counts as input. CircuitPython constrains what you can compute. OSC constrains the format of the data.

These are not obstacles. They are the material conditions out of which something emerges. The question is not how to eliminate constraints but how to turn them from limitative to enabling.

  • What in your practice functions as gravity — the resistance you push against?
  • What would your work look like if that constraint were removed?
W01B

Epistemic Tools
Introduction

"Instruments have a life of their own. They do not merely follow theory; often they determine theory, because instruments determine what is possible, and what is possible determines to a large extent what can be thought."
W01B — Core Claim

Tools Are Not Neutral

If making is already a mode of thinking, then the instrument you make with is already shaping the kinds of thought that are possible.

Choosing TouchDesigner over Processing, Max/MSP over SuperCollider, a capacitive sensor over a camera — these are not just technical decisions. They are epistemological ones.

The choice of instrument is not neutral. It conditions what questions you can ask, what results you can produce, and — eventually — what you consider beautiful, interesting, or true.

W01B — Don Ihde

How Instruments Relate to the World

01

Embodiment Relations

You act through the instrument. It recedes. You experience the world, not the tool. (The practiced musician and the keys.)

02

Hermeneutic Relations

The instrument produces data you must interpret. A spectrograph, a sensor reading, a heat map. The tool mediates by translating.

03

Alterity Relations

The instrument becomes a quasi-other. You engage with it, not through it. Generative systems, interactive installations.

These modalities are tendencies, not fixed categories. Any practice oscillates between them.

W01B — Ihde

Amplification-Reduction

Every instrument amplifies certain features of the world and reduces others. No exception.

Instrument
Amplifies / Reduces
Microphone
Pressure-wave events in a frequency range / everything else
MIDI controller
Discrete pitch + velocity events / continuous, gestural sound
MPR121 sensor
Conductivity at 12 points / pressure, texture, temperature, gesture
Your DAW
Timeline-based regions, channels, automation / ?

The danger is not that tools reduce — they must. The danger is forgetting that they do.

W01B — Latour / Bowker & Star

Black Boxes

"The more naturalized the object becomes, the more unquestioning the relationship of the community to it; the more invisible the contingent and historical circumstances of its birth, the more it sinks into the community's routinely forgotten memory." — Bowker & Star

The tools you're most fluent with are the most dangerous. Fluency produces blindness.

The instrument you've used for years is the instrument whose assumptions you've most thoroughly absorbed into your practice without noticing.

  • What's the most black-boxed tool in your practice?
  • When did you last open it?
W01B — Agre

Computational Imperialism

"A computer, then, does not simply have an instrumental use in a given site of practice; the computer is frequently about that site in its very design." — Philip Agre, Toward a Critical Technical Practice

Computers don't just represent activity — they restructure it. A DAW doesn't just record music. It restructures music-making around timelines, discrete regions, channels, automation curves.

These are models of music built by engineers with particular assumptions about what music is.

Epistemic Tools Entanglement
W01B — Agre

The Split Identity

Craft foot

Fluency. Building. The practiced hand that reaches for the tool without thinking. The expertise that closes black boxes so you can work fast.

Critique foot

Questioning. Opening. The reflexive attention that re-examines assumptions the craft foot has naturalized. The voice that asks why this tool, why these defaults.

The split identity does not resolve. It is the ongoing condition of critical technical practice — not a phase you pass through on the way to having all the answers.

W01C — Agre: Intermediation

Naming Is a Philosophical Act

When designing your OSC namespace, you have to decide what to call your data:

Option
Problem
/sensor/capacitive_threshold_event/ch/7
Too technical — reveals the mechanism, hides the body
/presence_of_hand/right/index
Too phenomenological — claims more than the sensor knows
/sensor/touch/7
Intermediation — bridges the technical and the lived

Your first OSC schema is a philosophical document. What you choose to name your data channels reveals what you think the interaction is.

W01C — Research Example

Don't Play in My Hair

Capacitive touch electrodes embedded in a sculptural hair object. QT Py reads the MPR121 → OSC → Max/MSP triggers sound and visual response based on which electrodes are touched.

The sensor models touch as 12 threshold events. The sculpture is about touching Black hair — a gesture that carries enormous social, political, and intimate charge. The sensor cannot feel what that touch means.

The gap between what the electrode reads (conductance shift at electrode 7) and what the audience experiences (touching something they were never supposed to touch) is not a technical failure. It's the piece.

W01C — Exercise

Do Your Epistemic Inventory

Pick one tool you already use in your practice. Any tool. Ask:

  • What does this tool amplify? What becomes easy, visible, measurable?
  • What does this tool reduce? What disappears, gets rounded off?
  • What world does it assume exists? What implicit model of activity, body, or material does its design encode?
  • Who designed those assumptions, and for what context?

This exercise is the foundation of ASN 2 — Critical Technical Journal. It begins this week.

Week 01 — Key Terms

Vocabulary

Research-Creation

Activity at the prebifurcation point where making and thinking are co-constitutive, not sequential.

Immanent Critique

Inhabiting complicity and making it turn; critique from inside the system, not from outside it.

Enabling Constraint

A limitation that, through interaction with other forces, generates emergence rather than just restriction.

Epistemic Tool

An instrument that is a knowledge-making artifact; tools determine what is possible and thus what can be thought.

Amplification-Reduction

Every tool amplifies certain features and reduces others. No tool is neutral. (Ihde)

Black Box

A naturalized object whose internal contingency has become invisible through repeated use. (Latour)
