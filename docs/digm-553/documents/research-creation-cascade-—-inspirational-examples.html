↓
Isadora — software they built themselves

William Forsythe

Synchronous Objects for One Flat Thing, reproduced

Choreographic thinking doesn't disappear when the dance ends. Can movement organize things other than bodies? What other forms can choreographic structure take?

Dance performance → motion tracking → relational + structural data

Cuing patterns, spatial alignments, timing relationships extracted from a recorded piece of choreography

Camera + motion capture
↓
Custom visualization software (OSU ACCAD + The Forsythe Company)
↓
89 interactive data objects — animations, scores, tools built from movement

Onyx Ashanti

beatjazz

The Black body as a fully sovereign instrument. Full-spectrum musical autonomy — composition, performance, improvisation — all in and from the body. No stage, no studio, no separation between thinking and sounding.

Hand position + breath + full-body movement → continuous gesture + breath data

The body IS the instrument and the interface — not a metaphor but literally. Every movement is a performance decision.

DIY handheld controllers + breath mouthpiece sensor + smartphone
↓
Pure Data + custom OSC routing
↓
Full live looping, synthesis, and arrangement — performed anywhere, no infrastructure needed

George Lewis

Voyager (1987–)

What would it mean for a machine to improvise in a Black musical tradition. Not mimicking it, but thinking with it? Can software carry cultural memory and agency?

Live instrumental performance → MIDI: note, velocity, timing, pitch, density data

The data is musical behavior — not just notes but phrasing, energy, direction, momentum. The machine listens to all of it.

Any MIDI instrument (trombone, saxophone, piano)
↓
Voyager — custom Lisp-based AI improvisation system (built at STEIM, Amsterdam)
↓
Virtual improvising orchestra: responds to the player AND generates independently

HKW | The Disappearance of Music: George Lewis: Timelike Weave | Voyager

Imogen Heap

Mi.Mu Gloves

Performance should be fully embodied. Why do singers stand static at a mic while all the sonic shaping happens offstage, invisible? The gesture should be the instrument.

Hand + finger position, orientation, gesture → flex sensor readings + accelerometer/gyroscope data

A vocabulary of gesture mapped to a vocabulary of sound. Moving a wrist is composing.

Custom gloves (flex sensors, IMUs, WiFi module)
↓
Max/MSP + custom gesture mapping software
↓
Real-time synthesis, effects, looping, and arrangement — the hands become the studio

David Rokeby

Very Nervous System (1982–1991)

What if the body could play an instrument not by touching it, but by moving through space? What is the instrument, if the instrument is the air itself?

Presence + movement → optical flow from video

The camera reads motion vectors — not what the body looks like, but how it is moving. Velocity and direction become the raw material.

Video camera + frame grabber (built 1982 — before Max/MSP existed)
↓
Custom C software → synthesizer control signals
↓
Movement generates sound in real time — no touch, no controller, no stage equipment

Rafael Lozano-Hemmer

Pulse Room (2006)

Every visitor who has been here is still present — their heartbeat still in the room. What would it mean to make presence accumulate rather than vanish?

The body at rest → pulse / heart rate → continuous biometric stream

The most intimate involuntary signal: a heartbeat. Data the body produces without thinking. Completely individual, completely universal.

Pulse oximeter sensors (visitors hold a handle)
↓
Custom real-time system
↓
300 incandescent bulbs — each pulsing at the rhythm of a past visitor. Newest arrival displaces the oldest heartbeat.

Echoes of Harmonic Threads

Black hair as a site of cultural memory, boundary, and desire. What happens when the hair object becomes a circuit — touch activating the politics of contact?

Movement + Afrofuturism → purpose built hydrophones as musical ←→ cultural ←→ scientific instruments

Kinect(v2) + Dancer + Designer
↓

↓
Unreal Engine + TouchDesigner → sound + image response
