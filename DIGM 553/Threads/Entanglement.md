# Entanglement

Practitioner, tool, and material do not interact as pre-formed, separate entities. They are mutually constituted through the practice itself. You don't bring a self to a tool — the self that acts is partly made by the act.

---

Barad's posthumanist performativity replaces *interaction* (between pre-existing things) with **intra-action** (the process through which distinct agencies emerge). An **apparatus** — for Barad, any material-discursive arrangement that makes knowledge possible — doesn't just measure or represent phenomena; it participates in producing them. The researcher, the instrument, and the phenomenon are all co-constituted through the apparatus.

For this course, that means: you are not a stable subject using a neutral tool to study an external object. You are entangled with your hardware, your software, your materials, and the site of your practice. These entanglements are not metaphors — they are structural features of how knowledge gets made. Documenting them honestly is part of what the Critical Technical Journal is for.

Camus & Vinck extend this into digital materiality: the digital is not immaterial. It is made of matter — servers, cables, electricity, screen light. Every "digital" thing has a thickness, a weight, a set of dependencies. The click is a material event. The file is an inscription on physical media. Recognizing this thickness is how you stop treating your tools as transparent windows onto some other reality.

## Key Questions

- What are you entangled with in your current project — not just using, but mutually constituted by? What would change about the project if that entanglement changed?
- Barad replaces "interaction" with "intra-action." What's the difference? Try to locate an intra-action — a moment where the boundaries between you, your tool, and your material were genuinely unclear.
- Where does the "digital" in your practice have material weight — consume power, require infrastructure, produce heat, leave traces?
- If your apparatus (the whole material-discursive arrangement of your practice) is not just measuring phenomena but helping produce them — what phenomena is yours producing?
