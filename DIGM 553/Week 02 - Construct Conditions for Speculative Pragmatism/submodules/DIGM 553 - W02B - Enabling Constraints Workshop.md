# W02B — Enabling Constraints Workshop

> *A limitation that, through interaction with other forces, generates emergence rather than just restriction.*

The goal today is to do our best to *feel* and operate an ***enabling constraint***. We're going to work with what we brought and see what happens when we push on its limits.

## The Gravity Example

For Manning & Massumi: gravity is implacable. You can't dance without it. You can jump without it. There are many aspects of our daily lives and activates that would be impossible without it. Gravity thus, *enables* the possible. 

Now, take the jump. Add horizontal momentum and you get an arc: a *collaboration between two co-causes*. Neither gravity nor momentum alone makes the jump. The constraint is generative *because* of its resistance.

**The question this opens:** 
- What's the gravity in your object? 
- What does it resist that you could push against?

## Workshop Structure

### Round 1 — Describe Without Defaulting (15 min)

Place your object on the shared surface. Describe it — but **you cannot use the words "material" or "art."**

Instead, try:
- What does it resist? What does it yield to?
- What does it amplify? What does it reduce?
- What can *not* be done with it? (That's your starting constraint.)
- What does it do to the thing closest to it?

---

### Round 2 — Convert a Limitative Constraint (10 min)

Pick one thing your object *cannot* do. One real limitation.

Now: **what second thing (a co-cause) could convert that limitation into generative potential?**

The co-cause can be:
- Another object or material
- A rule or instruction
- A body position or gesture
- A sensor or signal chain
- A time constraint

Write down the pair: `[limitation] + [co-cause] → [what becomes possible]`

*You're designing an enabling constraint.*

---

### Round 3 — Put It in the Signal Chain (10 min)

Pick one object from the table. [Conceptually] connect your object to a sensor. (or if you have the stuff, you could actually do it)

**At the moment of connection:** pause. Ask:
- What just happened to this object? What did it *become*?
- What does the sensor now "know" about the object? What doesn't it know?
- What's being amplified? What's being reduced?

Write 2–3 sentences: *what did the object become when it entered the signal chain?*

*This is the physical-digital entanglement moment — a foreign object enters a circuit and both things change.*

---

### Round 4 — Second Descriptions (10 min) [Optional]

Return to your original object. Describe it again — same no-"material"/no-"art" constraint from Round 1, but now you've been through the workshop.

Did your description change? If yes: what changed it? If no: what does that tell you about how you're relating to this object?

Group discussion: what's the enabling constraint of the workshop itself?

## Examples — Works That Use Constraints as Generative Logic

A few works where the constraint *is* the piece, not an obstacle to it:

**Alvin Lucier — *I Am Sitting in a Room* (1969)**
The room's acoustic resonance is the constraint. Lucier records himself speaking, plays it back into the room, records that, plays it back again,  until the room's frequencies overtake his voice entirely. The limitation (imperfect playback in a reverberant space) becomes the compositional process.
→ [Listen / read](https://www.ubu.com/sound/lucier.html)

**Neri Oxman — Material Ecology / Silk Pavilion (2013)**
The constraint is the silkworm's biology:  it can only spin within certain temperature and humidity ranges, following pheromone traces. Oxman designs the armature; the worms design the surface. The biological constraint shapes the architecture.
→ [MIT Media Lab documentation](https://oxman.com/projects/silk-pavilion)

**Tarek Atoui — *The Reverse Collection* (2016)**
Atoui works with Deaf and hearing-impaired communities to design instruments that bypass conventional acoustic listening. The "constraint" of non-standard hearing becomes the condition for new instrument forms.
→ [Frieze profile](https://www.frieze.com/article/tarek-atoui)

**Pauline Oliveros — Deep Listening practice**
Not a single work, but a practice built around the constraint of *duration and attention*: listening so slowly and deeply that normally unnoticed sounds become material. The constraint is temporal: you are not allowed to move on.
→ [Deep Listening Institute](https://www.deeplistening.rpi.edu/)

**Torkwase Dyson — *Black Compositional Thought* (ongoing)**
Dyson uses the constraint of a fixed geometric vocabulary — arcs, rectangles, horizontal planes — to think spatial liberation for Black bodies. The limitation of the vocabulary becomes the condition for specificity.
→ [Artist site](https://www.torkwasedyson.com/)

## Questions to Bring Into Discussion

- When did a constraint in your practice stop being frustrating and start being useful? What changed?
- What would it mean to *design* your constraints before you design your content?
- Is there a constraint in your current project that's limitative? Could you convert it?
