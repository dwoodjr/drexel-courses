Camus & Vinck on digital materiality: digital objects have texture, resistance, and agency. Moving a project into digital form is a transformation. Something is gained and something is lost.

## From the Reading — Key Moves in Camus & Vinck

**Becoming digital.** Moving something into a digital system creates a new object with different properties. The original and the digital version have different properties. Something is gained; something is lost. They are two different things.

**Digital objects have thickness.** They break. They resist. They require maintenance. They behave differently across platforms, versions, and conditions. Treating them as transparent or frictionless hides the labor and the loss involved in maintaining that smoothness.

**Scripts and inscriptions.** Every digital tool has a script: embedded assumptions about valid inputs, valid operations, valid outputs. When you use a tool, you partially accept its script. Part of critical technical practice is knowing when you're complying with the script and when you're pushing against it.

**Matter-network.** Your project is a set of relations between things, maintained through active labor. The sensor, the cable, the library, the code, the laptop, the power supply, the body using it: none of these alone is "the project." The project lives in the connections between them. Change any element and you change the network.

## Discussion — Closing Camus & Vinck

- Where in your project has "becoming digital" created a new object rather than a copy? What did you gain? What is no longer there?
- What is the script of your primary tool? What does it assume you want to do? Are you working with that assumption or against it?
- Name every element in your matter-network right now. Which connections are stable? Which are fragile? Where is the maintenance labor concentrated?
