## Journal 2 — Feedback

### What's working

The signal chain matrix is executed at a high level. What makes it work is that you are applying the four research domains (Sensory Worlding, Material Memory, Fugitive Speculation, Intimate Witnessing) not just to the MPR121, but consistently across the full chain — MPR121 > QTPy serial protocol> TouchDesigner. That longitudinal reading is exactly the point of the exercise, and your insight that "each tool amplifies each other's reductions" is the most sophisticated observation in either journal. That is a genuinely original synthesis.

The **Matrix Insights** paragraph is some of the strongest insights from both of your journals, so far I really enjoy this: "We are both adapting to each other," the mutual co-constitution of sensor behavior and bodily habit is a precise, embodied, and phenomenologically rich observation. This is the kind of observations practice-based and phenomenological methods in digital media looks for. It also quietly references something Agre is pointing at: the body is not pre-given to the system, it is shaped by the system's demands. It seems you are finding this yourself through direct interaction.

The **WorldView Statement** shows real command of the framework's language. "This apparatus believes the world is made of conductive materials" is the kind of personification-as-critique the exercise is asking for. And your follow-through, mentioning that the protocol and TouchDesigner share the same "cosmology," is a very fitting close to the argument.

The final line is the one I want you to carry into the next phase of work: *"what the system cannot invite into the chain, and what I might want to focus on instead."* That is your Speculative Proposal question, right there. It just needs a bit more “narrative” specificity.

### Something to push on

The Fugitive Speculation column is the one place where the thinking goes slightly thin. "Glitches, lag, and irregular values could become part of the experience" is a well-known move in glitch aesthetics. And not so say that those are invalid points, but I'd push there just a bit, so you to make it more *specific to your practice*. What would it mean for *you*, with your tendencies and your background, to design with hesitation as data? What do those cultural objects or rituals say bout that? The speculative column is where the framework asks you to be a little reckless and weird. So embrace that some.

**🟡 Yellow Hat — Optimism and Value**
These journals show a practitioner actively revising her self-model in real time, which is rare and valuable. The recursive loop — catch the habit, apply the tool, notice what the tool reveals about the habit — is already a research-creation methodology in miniature. The WorldView Statement in Journal 2 is publishable-quality critical writing for a second-week submission.

**🟢 Green Hat — Creativity and New Ideas**
The observation that the sensor, protocol, and software share a "cosmology" of measurability is generative. It suggests a potential project direction: what would an apparatus look like that is *designed around what this chain cannot detect*? Hesitation, ambiguity, non-conductive contact, social context, emotion — these become not just limitations but the *design space* for a counter-apparatus. The Boundary Object for the Speculative Proposal could be precisely this: an object that sits at the edge of what the signal chain can and cannot invite.

**🔴 Red Hat — Emotions and Intuition**
There is a palpable sense of relief in Journal 1 when the enabling constraints exercise clicks — "it tied perfectly" — and a growing confidence across the two submissions. Journal 2 feels more settled in its language, less anxious about getting it "right." That arc is healthy and worth naming. Ghada's self-awareness about her own cognitive habits (system-thinking, output-orientation) reads as honest rather than self-critical, which is the right register.

**🔵 Blue Hat — Process and Meta-Thinking**
Ghada is managing the process of her own learning well. She revisited the Enabling Constraints exercise when she caught herself defaulting — that's metacognitive self-regulation in the service of the methodology. The journal format is being used correctly: not as a summary of readings but as a site of active thinking. Going forward, the one process note is to let the cultural/emotional threads develop rather than bracket them. That is where the Speculative Proposal will likely come from.

## Looking Ahead: Boundary Object & Speculative Proposal
Feedback in general:

Both journals demonstrate solid factual command of the course materials and hardware. The MPR121 threshold values (touch ~12–20, release ~6–10) cited in Journal 2(1) show your hands-on engagement with the hardware. Manning and Massumi, Agre, and Magnusson are all engaged substantively and with accurate paraphrase.

There is a thread running through both journals that you keep almost-naming: the gap between what the signal chain can detect and the kinds of interaction (emotional, cultural, embodied, hesitant) that it cannot. You mention "cultural perspective" and "familiar or cultural objects." I want to you stay in that space a bit longer and try and push yourself to name those things more concretely. I know that can be a challenge, but i feel it will be worthwhile to try. I think this can really help lead you into the core of the Speculative Proposal and the project. Is there historical context to draw from in trying to define this “gap”? Political? Economic, or ecological? What are those cultural object and the social rituals around them, specifically?

I'd like you to go there. For the Speculative Proposal and its Boundary Object, consider: what is the cultural register of touch that the MPR121 erases? What kinds of contact, in your own experience or context, carry meaning that conductivity perhaps cannot carry? And are there ways to “manipulate” it to? You also mention the tension in how you default to working and how this course is asking you to work. Those are different commitments that can also be expressed in defining the Boundary Object.
