# Epistemic Tool Audit Workshop

This is the in-class version. The formal written version becomes **Journal Entry 1**, due before Week 4.

See the full template and instructions here: [[../../Documents/Tool Audit — Template + Guide|Tool Audit — Template + Guide]]

---

## How It Works

Pick **one software tool** and **one hardware tool** from your current practice kit (or the ones you hope/plan to use in the class).  
Try to pick:
- The one you use most. 
- The one you've stopped thinking about.

Work through the audit questions. Then: find where the two tools' worldviews *conflict* or *compound*. ***That's where things get interesting.***

---

## In-Class Protocol

**15 min** — individual audit (both tools, quietly)
**15 min** — pair share: what surprised you? what did the tool assume that you never examined?
**15 min** — full group: collect the most surprising assumptions on the board. What patterns emerge?

---

## The Questions (short version)

**What does it know?**
What model of the world is this tool built on? What does it assume about inputs, outputs, users, bodies, time?

**What does it amplify?**
What becomes easy, fast, expressive when you use this tool?

**What does it reduce?**
What gets rounded off, hidden, made impossible?

**Who made the defaults?**
Who designed this, and for what context? Who were they imagining as the user?

**Where do you hit the wall?**
When does the tool resist what you're trying to do? What does the resistance feel like?

**What have you stopped questioning?**
What have you naturalized (made transparent or made invisible) through fluency?
