# Magnusson — Epistemic Tools

Every tool you use is already arguing something. It has opinions baked in,  about what counts as data, what counts as sound, what a "good" output looks like. Magnusson calls these **epistemic tools**: instruments that don't just do things, they *know* things. And what they know shapes what you can think.

---

## The Core Claim

> *"Instruments determine what is possible, and what is possible determines to a large extent what can be thought."*

TouchDesigner thinks in networks and real-time data flow. Max/MSP thinks much the same way, but more discreetly with time via messages and patches. Unity thinks in (game) objects with update loops. 

Each of those is a set of propositions about how the world is/should be organized. When you choose a tool, you're not picking a neutral vessel. You're entering a particular way of understanding what an interactive experience even *is*.

---

## Amplification + Reduction

Every tool amplifies something and reduces something else. The DAW amplifies the timeline, linearity, and arrangement. This idea that music has a start and end. It reduces improvisation, emergence, the idea that music is a living event. Neither is wrong. But the reduction is always there, whether you notice it or not.

The question isn't "is my tool limiting me?" It always is. The question is: *what is it limiting, and is that a problem for what I'm trying to make?*

---

## Industry Pulse — How Studios Work With This

[**Bluecadet**](https://www.bluecadet.com) builds museum and cultural experiences at scale. When they choose a platform — Unity, TouchDesigner, custom web — they're not just choosing a tool for one project. They're choosing an *episteme* that shapes how they interpret every brief that comes in. Their tool stack encodes what an "interactive" experience can be. Worth asking: what does Bluecadet's stack foreclose?

[**Meow Wolf**](https://meowwolf.com)  scales experiential art into commercial spaces. Their challenge is a version of this problem too: how do you encode wonder as a repeatable, maintainable system and scale? What do their tools know about wonder that might be... wrong?

**[BRDG studios](https://brdg.co)**  works smaller and more bespoke. Less infrastructure, more contingency. Their tool choices feel more situational, which means the epistemic assumptions shift project to project. Is that freedom, or is it just a different kind of constraint?

## Ihde's Three Modalities (Quick Reference)

- **Embodiment relation** — you act *through* the tool; it recedes into the background. (You don't think about the hammer, you think about the nail.)
- **Hermeneutic relation** — the tool *produces data* you have to read and interpret. (The sensor gives you a number; you figure out what it means.)
- **Alterity relation** — you engage *with* the tool as a quasi-other, a presence you're in dialogue with. (Somax2. The model that surprises you.)

Which one describes your current relationship to your primary tool? When does it shift?
