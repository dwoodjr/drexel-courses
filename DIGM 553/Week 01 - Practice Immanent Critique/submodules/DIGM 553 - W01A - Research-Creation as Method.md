# W01A — Research-Creation as Method

> *"Making would already be thinking-in-action, and conceptualization a practice in its own right."*
> — Manning & Massumi, *Thought in the Act*

---

## The Problem We're Naming

Most of what we call "research" and most of what we call "creative practice" assume they are separate, sequential, activities. Research produces knowledge. Then, practice produces work. The knowledge informs the work. The work illustrates the knowledge. This is the typical “institutional model” of knowledge production. It is also the model we're going to spend the rest of this course systematically inhabiting and turning.

**Research-creation**, as described by the authors Manning & Massumi is an activity that occurs at what they call the *prebifurcation point* : the point just before research and creation diverge into the institutional structures that evaluate them by separate criteria. At that point, making and thinking are not distinct acts. They are two registers of the same act, happening simultaneously.

---

## What Immanent Critique Actually Means

The theme of Week 01 is "Practice Immanent Critique." Here's what that phrase means from the ground up.

An **immanent** critique does not stand *outside* a system to pass judgment. Instead, it works *from inside*. Id does so in order to inhabit the structure, understand its logic from within, and then find the pressure points where it might be turned. 

Manning & Massumi's definition:

> [!quote] "An immanent critique engages with new processes more than new products, from a constructivist angle. It seeks to energize new modes of activity, already in germ, that seem to offer a potential to escape or overspill ready-made channelings into the dominant value system. The strategy of immanent critique is to inhabit one's complicity and make it turn — in the sense in which butter 'turns' to curd."

Notice what this requires: *inhabiting complicity*. You are not exempt from the system you critique. You work from within it, on its tools, in its institutions, using its grants and its grades. Immanent critique means doing that *while* turning it, not waiting until you're free of it to begin this form of practice.

***So, let’s analyze this from our shared perspective.*** **For this course:** 
- We are working at a university. 
- We use tools (TouchDesigner, Max/MSP, Ableton, Python) that carry embedded worldviews. 
- We receive grades.

All of this is our complicity. 
*Immanent critique is the method by which we make that complicity productive rather than just reproductive.*

## The Built-In Contradiction (and Why It's Not a Problem)

Manning & Massumi acknowledge a tension: the institutional framing of research-creation — especially in the context of digital media and media studies — pushes it toward "formatting artistic activity for more directly economic forms of delivery to stakeholders." Digital media works often get funded in part *because* they "employ the same technological platforms as the increasingly computerized economy."

For us, this is a friction of our practice (and a contradiction that is not easily resolved). Our work is to neither ignore this friction nor be paralyzed by it. Instead we should treat it as what Agre would call a **hassle**: a recurring friction that is itself data about the structure of the field.

## Three Distinctions to Internalize

### 1. Process vs. Product

The danger Manning & Massumi identify: research-creation "settles into a communication model revolving around the delivery of results." The goal instead is what they call "an interpenetration of processes rather than a communication of products."

What does that mean in practice? 

When you work on a project in this course, you are not primarily *building* something that will be assessed. You are *practicing* something. Developing a relationship to your tools, your materials, and your questions. The artifact becomes secondary, it is the record of that practice, not the point of it.

### 2. Conditioning vs. Framing

These are technically different acts. 
- **Framing** is setting up a structure: here are the rules, here are the constraints, here is the question, go. 
- **Conditioning** is bringing co-causes into interaction such that something *emerges* that neither cause alone would produce.

> [!quote] "Conditioning consists in bringing co-causes into interaction, such that the participation yields something different from either acting alone… Emergent process, dedicated to the singular occurrence of the new, agitates inventively in an open field. Programmed organization, on the other hand, functions predictably in a bounded frame and lends itself to reproduction."

In this class, ***we are going to try to condition rather than frame***. We should strive to set up initial conditions; not attempt to predetermine outcomes.

### 3. Enabling Constraints

The concept that connects it all: **enabling constraints**.

An enabling constraint is positive in its dynamic effect even though it may be limiting in its form. The classic example: gravity is a constraint on dance, but it enables the jump. Without gravity to push against, there is no arc, no lift, no landing. The jump is a collaboration between the dancer and gravity — two co-causes whose interaction produces something neither does alone.

> [!quote] "In our experience, unconstrained interaction rarely yields worthwhile effects."

This applies directly to the hardware and software in this course. The MPR121 sensor *constrains* what counts as input. CircuitPython *constrains* what you can compute on the microcontroller. OSC protocol *constrains* the format of the data. These are not obstacles — they are the material conditions out of which something emerges. The question is not how to eliminate constraints but how to turn them from limitative to enabling.

## Techniques of Relation

Manning & Massumi introduce the concept of **techniques of relation**: "devices for catalyzing and modulating interaction" that comprise "a domain of practice in their own right."

> Techniques of relation are are structural: they shape how things interact, how processes co-evolve, how different elements of a practice can be brought into productive contact. 

They operate in two modes:
1. **Techniques that set up initial conditions** — propitious starting points for emergence
2. **Techniques that modulate an event as it moves through its phases** — improvised steering without predetermined destination

A key property: 
> [!quote] "Techniques as we understand them do not depend exclusively on the content of the practices but move across their respective processes at the site of their potential multiplication." 

A technique is transferable. It can move from sound to movement to code to pedagogy.

**Assignment connection:** Part of your ASN 2 (Critical Technical Journal) practice is to identify the techniques of relation already operating in your practice in order to begin consciously designing new ones.

## Opening Provocation

**If every practice is already a mode of thought, what is your current practice thinking?**

Manning & Massumi open *Thought in the Act* with: 

> [!quote] "Every practice is a mode of thought, already in the act. To dance: a thinking in movement. To paint: a thinking through color. To perceive in the everyday: a thinking of the world's varied ways of affording itself."

So for us: 
- To program is a thinking in what? 
- To route audio signals is a thinking in what? 
- To touch a capacitive sensor is a thinking in what? 
- To capture field recordings is a thinking in what?

Right now, these are more rhetorical questions, but your first journal entries will begin to answer them, literally.

## Key Terms

- **Research-creation** — activity at the prebifurcation point where making and thinking are co-constitutive, not sequential
- **Immanent critique** — inhabiting complicity and making it turn; critique from inside, not outside
- **Enabling constraint** — a limitation that, through interaction with other forces, generates emergence rather than just restriction
- **Conditioning** — bringing co-causes into interaction toward open, emergent outcomes (vs. framing toward predetermined ones)
- **Techniques of relation** — devices for catalyzing and modulating interaction; transferable across practices
