# The Prebifurcation Point

Most institutional models treat research and creative practice as *sequential* and *separate*: research produces knowledge, practice illustrates it.

**Research-creation** is an attempt to name the activity that happens before this split. Where making and thinking are not distinct acts but two registers of the same act, happening simultaneously.

> Manning & Massumi: activity at the **prebifurcation point** — before research and creation diverge into institutional structures that evaluate them by separate criteria.

<span style="font-family:'Courier New',monospace; font-size:0.58em; color:#ffb300; letter-spacing:0.1em;">W01A — Immanent Critique</span>

# Inhabiting Complicity

> "An immanent critique engages with new processes more than new products… The strategy of immanent critique is to inhabit one's complicity and make it turn — in the sense in which butter 'turns' to curd."
>
> — Manning & Massumi

We work *within* the university, with commercial tools, toward grades. That is our complicity. Immanent critique means making that complicity productive. We do not wait until we're free of it to begin practice.

# The Productive Limit

> "In our experience, unconstrained interaction rarely yields worthwhile effects."
>
> — Manning & Massumi

The MPR121 sensor constrains what counts as input. CircuitPython constrains what you can compute. OSC constrains the format of the data.

These are **the material conditions from which something emerges**. We do not aim to eliminate constraints, but turn them from limitative to enabling.

- What in your practice functions as *gravity* — the resistance you push against?
- What would your work look like if that constraint were removed?

# Tools Are Not Neutral

If making is already a mode of thinking, then the instrument you make *with* is already shaping the kinds of thought that are possible.

Choosing TouchDesigner over Processing, Max/MSP over SuperCollider, a capacitive sensor over a camera. 
These are **epistemological** decisions just as much as practical or technical.

The choice of instrument is never neutral. It conditions what questions you can ask, what results you can produce, and — eventually — what you consider beautiful, interesting, or true.

# How Instruments Relate to the World

<div style="display:grid; grid-template-columns:1fr 1fr 1fr; gap:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em;">

<div style="border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.07); padding:0.9em;">
<span style="font-family:'Courier New',monospace; font-size:0.6em; color:#ffb300;">01</span>

**Embodiment Relations**

You act *through* the instrument. It recedes. You experience the world, not the tool. (The practiced musician and the keys.)
</div>

<div style="border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.07); padding:0.9em;">
<span style="font-family:'Courier New',monospace; font-size:0.6em; color:#ffb300;">02</span>

**Hermeneutic Relations**

The instrument produces data you must *interpret*. A spectrograph, a sensor reading, a heat map. The tool mediates by translating.
</div>

<div style="border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.07); padding:0.9em;">
<span style="font-family:'Courier New',monospace; font-size:0.6em; color:#ffb300;">03</span>

**Alterity Relations**

The instrument becomes a “quasi-other”. You engage *with* it, not through it. Generative systems, interactive installations.
</div>

</div>

<span style="font-size:0.7em; color:#d4a844;">These modalities are tendencies, not fixed categories. Any practice oscillates between them.</span>

# Amplification-Reduction

Every instrument amplifies certain features of the world and reduces others. No exception.

| Instrument | Amplifies / Reduces |
|---|---|
| Microphone | Pressure-wave events in a frequency range / everything else |
| MIDI controller | Discrete pitch + velocity events / continuous, gestural sound |
| MPR121 sensor | Conductivity at 12 points / pressure, texture, temperature, gesture |
| Your DAW | Timeline-based regions, channels, automation / ? |

<span style="font-size:0.7em; color:#d4a844;">The danger is not that tools reduce, they must in order to function. The danger is *forgetting* what they reduce.</span>

# Black Boxes

> "The more naturalized the object becomes, the more unquestioning the relationship of the community to it; the more invisible the contingent and historical circumstances of its birth, the more it sinks into the community's routinely forgotten memory."
>
> — Bowker & Star

The tools you're most fluent with are the most dangerous. **Fluency produces blindness.**

The instrument you've used for years is the instrument whose assumptions you've most thoroughly absorbed into your practice without noticing.

- What's the most black-boxed tool in your practice?
- When did you last open it?

# Computational Imperialism

> "A computer, then, does not simply have an instrumental use in a given site of practice; the computer is frequently *about* that site in its very design."
>
> — Philip Agre, *Toward a Critical Technical Practice*

Computers, on top of representing activity. also *restructure* it. A DAW (while used for recording and production), fundamentally restructures music-making around timelines, discrete regions, channels, automation curves.

These are models of music built by engineers with particular assumptions about what music *is*.

# The Split Identity

<div style="display:grid; grid-template-columns:1fr 1fr; gap:1.2em; margin:1em 0;">

<div>

### Craft foot

Fluency. Building. The practiced hand that reaches for the tool without thinking. The expertise that closes black boxes so you can work fast.

</div>

<div>

### Critique foot

Questioning. Opening. The reflexive attention that re-examines assumptions the craft foot has naturalized. The voice that asks *why this tool, why these defaults*.

</div>

</div>

> The split identity never “resolves”. It is the ongoing condition of critical technical practice, not a phase of it.

# Naming Is a Philosophical Act

When designing your OSC namespace, you have to decide what to *call* your data:

| Option                                    | Problem                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| `/sensor/capacitive_threshold_event/ch/7` | Too technical: reveals the mechanism, hides the body     |
| `/presence_of_hand/right/index`           | Too phenomenological:  claims more than the sensor knows |
| `/sensor/touch/7`                         | Intermediation: bridges the technical and the lived      |

Your first OSC schema is a philosophical document. What you choose to name your data channels reveals what you think the interaction *is*.

# Do Your Epistemic Inventory

Pick one tool you already use in your practice. Any tool. Ask:

- What does this tool **amplify**? What becomes easy, visible, measurable?
- What does this tool **reduce**? What disappears, gets rounded off?
- What world does it **assume exists**? What implicit model of activity, body, or material does its design encode?
- **Who** designed those assumptions, and for what context?

<span style="font-size:0.72em; color:#d4a844;">This exercise is the foundation of ASN 2 — Critical Technical Journal. *It begins this week.*</span>

# Three Questions
to Carry

- **What does your tool amplify?** What becomes easy, visible, measurable?
- **What does your tool reduce?** What is systematically missing from what it captures?
- **Who designed the reduction?** The defaults in your software were chosen by someone with specific assumptions. What were they?
