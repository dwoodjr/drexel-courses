# Reading Guide — Week 01

> **Primary reading due Week 02.** Background readings recommended before or alongside class.

---

## Primary Reading

### Manning, E. & Massumi, B. — *Thought in the Act*: ***For Thought in the Act***
**Propositions 0–3** (pp. 83–97)
Minnesota University Press, 2014.

**Access:** [[Manning_Massumi_2014_TITA_6.pdf|Manning + Massumi – Though in the Act]]

---

#### How to Read It

Do not try to understand this text completely on a first read. For context, these are two authors who think that the form of writing itself should enact the argument being made; so it is written as such. It also may read as a bit “overly critical,” but it is important to understanding the context of their work. Let the reading wash over you once. Then read it again more slowly, with a highlighter or notebook.

**On the first pass:** Mark any sentence that feels like it's writing about your own practice, even if you can't yet say why. Don't worry about the vocabulary.

**On the second pass:** Look for these three things specifically:
- The word **technique** and how they're using it
- The distinction between **conditioning** and **framing**
- Any moment where they talk about what a *body* does (including a digital body)

---

#### Reading Questions

These are not quiz questions! Just points of entry to the conversation; points of inquiry and immobility, both. You need not provide written answers, but you should have, at least, *thought* about them.

1. Manning & Massumi say research-creation happens at the "prebifurcation point", a point before research and creation diverge into institutional structures. **Where is that point in your current practice?** Is there a moment in your workflow when the thinking and the making are still the same thing? When does the split happen?

2. They distinguish between *framing* a project (setting up a structure in advance) and *conditioning* one (bringing co-causes into interaction so something can emerge). **Think of a project you've worked on.** Was it framed or conditioned, maybe even both, at different stages? What did that feel like from the inside?

3. "An enabling constraint is positive in its dynamic effect, even though it may be limiting in its form." **What enabling constraints are already active in your tools, materials, and methods?** Which ones do you want to be more deliberate about?

4. "Unconstrained interaction rarely yields worthwhile effects." Do you agree? **What is the minimum constraint needed for something interesting to happen in your practice?**

5. Manning & Massumi warn against research-creation settling into "a communication model revolving around the delivery of results." **How does your current program or discipline evaluate your work?** Where does that evaluation model push back against research-creation?

## Coming to Class Prepared

You don't need to have fully digested any of this before Week 02. You need to:

- Have **read** Manning & Massumi Propositions 0–3 at least once
- Have your **(Im)material Potluck** contribution ready: see [[(Im)material Potluck — Prep|(Im)material Potluck — Prep]]
- Have at least one **question or point of friction** from the reading to bring into the room

The discussions are only as good as what each person brings to it. Your confusion is just as valuable as your insight, so bring both! :)
